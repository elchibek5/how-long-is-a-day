Step 1 · Noon

The Sun is straight overhead

Our person stands on the side of the planet that faces the Sun. Their sight-line points right at it: that is noon.

Step 2 · One spin

Spin once, all the way round

The planet turns a full 360° and points back at the same distant star. But it has also moved along its orbit, so the Sun is no longer overhead.

Step 3 · The geometry

The same angle, twice

The start sight-line and today’s sight-line are parallel. The line to the Sun crosses both, so the angle the planet moved, θ, appears again at the planet.

Step 4 · Noon again

Turn a little more

To face the Sun again the planet must turn the extra θ. One solar day is 360° + θ of spin.

Step 5 · Earth

On Earth, about four minutes

Earth moves about 1° along its orbit each day, and turning that extra degree takes just under four minutes.

Step 6 · Venus

Spinning backwards

Venus spins the other way. Turning against its orbit, it meets the Sun early: a solar day needs only 360° − θ.
